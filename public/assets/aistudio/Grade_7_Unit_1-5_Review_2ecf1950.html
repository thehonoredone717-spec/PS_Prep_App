Unit 1: Life Plans

Present & Future Tenses Review • Making Changes • Life Milestones • Phrases with up

10 Practice Questions

📘 Key Vocabulary & Expressions

Making Changes
make a resolution (decide to change), give up (stop doing something), take up (start a new hobby), break a bad habit / form a good habit, struggle with (find difficult), change your ways, do well.
Life Plans & Milestones
leave school → get a degree → travel the world → start a career → get promoted (get a better job in the same company) → settle down → start a family → retire.
Phrases with "up"
What's up? (What's the matter?) • be up to somebody (be someone's decision) • be up to something (doing something, often secret) • be up to it (capable of doing it) • up to [time] (as long as) • be up late (stay awake late).

⚙️ Grammar Focus

Present Tenses (Review)

Present Simple: routines, habits, facts, stative verbs (want, like, need).
Present Continuous: actions happening now or around now; annoying habits with always.
Present Perfect Simple: finished actions with present results (yet, already).
Present Perfect Continuous: actions that started in the past and continue now (for, since, all morning, lately).

"I've been writing invitations all morning, so my hand is tired."
Future Tenses (Review)

Present Continuous: fixed arrangements with a time/place.
going to: personal intentions & plans already decided.
will / won't: predictions, opinions (I think, maybe), and instant decisions.

"We're flying to Madrid on Monday." (arrangement) vs. "I'm going to study Maths at university." (intention)
⚠️ Get It Right! Do NOT use will for fixed arrangements already made: say "I'm seeing the dentist this afternoon" (not "I'll see").

Unit 2: Hard Times

Narrative Tenses • would vs. used to • Descriptive Verbs • Time Periods

10 Practice Questions

📘 Key Vocabulary & Expressions

Descriptive Action Verbs
rage (burn/spread violently), flee (run away from danger), scream (cry out loudly), grab (take hold of quickly), smash (break violently into pieces), demolish (destroy a building completely), dive (jump headfirst into water).
Fire & Disasters
break out (start suddenly, e.g., a fire), spread (move across an area), fight a fire, spark, flame, fuel, oxygen, catastrophe / disaster, lose one's life.
Time Periods
Present: nowadays, these days, in this day and age.
Recent past: not so long ago, a few decades ago, in the last century.
Distant past: in the Middle Ages, in the olden days, a long, long time ago.

⚙️ Grammar Focus

Narrative Tenses (Review)

Past Simple: completed events in a story.
Past Continuous: background actions in progress when another event interrupted.
Past Perfect Simple: an action that happened before another past event.
Past Perfect Continuous: an ongoing action happening before a past moment (focus on duration).

"He was tired because he had been running for hours."
used to vs. would

used to + base verb: past repeated actions AND past states (be, have, live, like).
would + base verb: ONLY past repeated actions/habits — NEVER with stative verbs!

✔ "I used to be overweight." (state — would is impossible here)
✔ "We would / used to play football after school." (action)
⚠️ Get It Right! Don't confuse used to (past habit) with usually (present habit): "These days I usually go to bed early."

Unit 3: What's in a Name?

Modals of Obligation, Advice & Permission • Making & Selling • Expressions with name

10 Practice Questions

📘 Key Vocabulary & Expressions

Making and Selling
manufacturer (company that makes goods), product (item made to sell), consumer (person who buys goods), target market (group a company wants to sell to), brand, logo (company symbol), image, chain (group of shops), advertisement.
Expressions with "name"
a big name / household name (very famous person or company) • stage name (performer's professional name) • make a name for yourself (become famous/respected) • the name of the game (the most important thing) • name the day (set a wedding date) • you name it (anything you can think of) • call someone names (insult someone).
Key Words in Context
approved (officially allowed), blend in (look similar to surroundings), distinguish (tell the difference), memorable, unique, permission.

⚙️ Grammar Focus

Obligation & Necessity

have to / must: strong obligation or rule.
don't have to: NO obligation (it is not necessary, you have a choice).
mustn't / can't: prohibition (it is forbidden / against the rules).

"It's a holiday tomorrow, so we don't have to go to school." vs. "You mustn't enter here."
Advice & Warnings

should(n't) / ought to: general advice or what is a good idea.
had better (not) + base verb: strong advice or warning about a specific situation (bad consequences if ignored).

"We'd better leave now, or we'll miss the train." (Note: no to after had better!)
⚠️ Get It Right! should = good idea / expected result; could = possibility or polite request ("If you want, you could bring some drinks").

Unit 4: Dilemmas

1st, 2nd & 3rd Conditionals • Time Conjunctions • wish / if only • Honesty & Decisions

10 Practice Questions

📘 Key Vocabulary & Expressions

Being Honest vs. Dishonest
Honest: tell the truth, own up (to something), be open about (something), do the right thing.
Dishonest: tell a lie, hide the truth, cheat, get away with (something — escape punishment).
Making a Decision
first thought (initial idea), think long and hard (consider very carefully), come to a decision / make up your mind (decide), reconsider (think again), change your mind (choose something different).
Phrases with "now"
now and again (occasionally) • just now (a moment ago) • now that (because of a new situation).

⚙️ Grammar Focus

Conditionals (1st, 2nd & 3rd)

1st Conditional (real/likely future): If + Present Simple, will / won't + verb.
2nd Conditional (unreal/imaginary present): If + Past Simple, would / wouldn't + verb.
3rd Conditional (unreal past / regrets): If + Past Perfect (had + V3), would(n't) have + V3.

"If I had studied harder, I would have got a better mark."
Time Conjunctions & wish / if only

Time conjunctions (when, as soon as, until, unless, if) are followed by Present Simple for future meaning (NOT will).
wish / if only + Past Simple / could: wish for a different present.
wish / if only + would: annoyance about someone's habit.

"I'll phone you as soon as she leaves." • "I wish my sister wouldn't keep taking my clothes."
⚠️ Get It Right! Never put would have in the if-clause: say "If you had contacted us..." (NOT "If you would have contacted us").

Unit 5: What a Story!

Defining & Non-Defining Relative Clauses • Comment Clauses with which • Book Genres & Story Elements

10 Practice Questions

📘 Key Vocabulary & Expressions

Types of Story (Genres)
science fiction novel (future/space/technology), historical novel (set in a real past period), crime novel (detectives & mysteries), horror story (scary/supernatural), romantic novel (love story), autobiography (life story written by the person), travel literature, poetry, short stories.
Elements of a Story
setting (time & place), plot (main events/storyline), character, hero / heroine (main good character), villain (bad character), dialogue (conversations), opening (beginning), ending.
Key Storytelling Words
anecdote (short funny/interesting true story), blockbuster (hugely successful film), capture one's attention, engage, pass down (share across generations), special effects, influential.

⚙️ Grammar Focus

Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Relative Pronouns: who (people), which (things), whose (possession), where (places), that (people/things in defining clauses only).
Defining (no commas): gives essential information to identify which person/thing we mean.
Non-Defining (with commas): gives extra information; removing it still leaves a complete sentence.

"My aunt, who is a poet, lives in London." (Non-defining — commas required)
Relative Clauses with "which" (Commenting on a whole clause)

We use a comma + which to refer back to the entire previous idea or situation.

"Their train arrived four hours late, which meant they missed the show."
⚠️ Get It Right! NEVER use that after a comma in a non-defining relative clause: say "I'm in Brazil, which is a beautiful country" (NOT "that is").
